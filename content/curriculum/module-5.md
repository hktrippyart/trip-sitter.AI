# Module 5: Integration & Sustaining Care

## Core Module Objectives

This module aims to assist volunteers and peer supporters in mastering integration practices following a guest's experience of intense emotional release, sensory overload, or heightened altered states.

The conclusion of an experience does not mark the end of the process; the days to weeks following an experience represent a golden window for cognitive processing, emotional digestion, and personal reflection.

This module focuses on training volunteers to support guests in digesting and translating their insights, emotional releases, and somatic experiences into their daily lives.

Additionally, it guides volunteers on how to gently and clearly perform the "closing of the container" (closing the support space) and assist guests in building long-term wellness and community support networks.

## 1. Fundamentals of Integration

### 1.1 Definition & Core Philosophy

Metabolizing & Grounding: Integration refers to the process of consciously organizing, "digesting," and weaving the insights, perspectives, unresolved emotions, or bodily sensations gained from a deep experience into daily life.

An Ongoing Process: Integration is not a one-time event or conversation, but a continuous, dynamic journey.

Capitalizing on the Window of Change: Following a powerful emotional release or deep personal experience, an individual's thinking patterns and habits are temporarily more flexible, providing a golden opportunity to break old behavioral patterns and establish healthier new routines.

### 1.2 Scope Boundaries: Peer Integration vs. Psychotherapy

Peer Integration Support — Role: Non-hierarchical companionship, peer listening, sharing general coping tools, and providing information on community resources. Focus: Helping guests build immediate self-care routines, reflect on current feelings, and offer non-directive support.

Psychotherapy & Clinical Care — Role: Deep trauma processing, psychopathological diagnosis, and structured long-term treatment conducted by licensed psychologists, clinical psychologists, social workers, or psychiatrists.

Psychotherapy & Clinical Care — Boundaries: Volunteers do not conduct trauma analysis, deep psychological interpretation, or clinical diagnosis. When a guest exhibits unresolved deep trauma or persistent psychological distress, volunteers should assist in referring them to professional medical or counseling services.

## 2. Domains of Integration Practice

Integration encompasses various aspects of an individual's life. Volunteers can utilize the following 7+1 dimensional framework, along with inquiry prompts and practical tools, to support guests in self-exploration. The eight domains are: Cognitive, Emotional, Somatic, Spiritual / Existential, Relational, Behavioral, Creative, and Societal / Global.

### 2.1 Cognitive Domain

Focus: Shifts in beliefs, organizing new perspectives. Prompts/Tools: "How has this experience shifted your perspective on a particular situation?", "Are there any new insights you'd like to write down?"

### 2.2 Emotional Domain

Focus: Digesting deep feelings, honoring remaining emotions. Prompts/Tools: "What emotions are still lingering in your body right now?", "How can we give those emotions a safe space to exist and resolve?"

### 2.3 Somatic Domain

Focus: Bodily awareness, nervous system regulation, basic physiological needs. Prompts/Tools: Prioritize adequate sleep, healthy nutrition, hydration, light stretching, or grounding physical practices (e.g., warm baths, gentle walking).

### 2.4 Spiritual / Existential Domain

Focus: Worldview, meaning of life, and sense of connectedness. Prompts/Tools: "How has this experience influenced your relationship with the world around you or your sense of purpose?"

### 2.5 Relational Domain

Focus: Personal boundaries, communication, and interpersonal relationships. Prompts/Tools: "Do you feel a desire to adjust boundaries with anyone in your life? How can we pause and reflect before making any sudden decisions?"

### 2.6 Behavioral Domain

Focus: Reshaping daily habits, setting small actionable steps. Prompts/Tools: Encourage setting "small and sustainable" goals (e.g., taking a 10-minute daily walk, journaling) rather than making immediate, drastic life choices.

### 2.7 Creative Domain

Focus: Non-verbal expression of experiences. Prompts/Tools: Encourage drawing, music-making, writing, dancing, creating vision boards, or spending time in nature.

### 2.8 Societal / Global Domain

Focus: Community involvement, environmental care, and social altruism. Prompts/Tools: "Did this experience spark a desire to get involved in your local community or contribute to a cause?"

## 3. Closing the Container & Personal Wellness Plan

When a peer support session or field shift is coming to an end, a clear and safe closure must be conducted.

### 3.1 Closing the Support Space (Container)

Clear Time Boundaries: Inform the guest of the remaining time in advance (e.g., "We have about 15 minutes left together, so we'll begin making a smooth transition to wrap up.").

Transitions & Rituals: Guide a few relaxing breaths or bring focus back to the physical feeling of feet planted on the floor. Express gratitude for the guest's trust and courage, while clearly stepping out of the "peer support" role and returning to everyday boundaries.

### 3.2 Personal Wellness Plan

Before the guest leaves, assist them in outlining a basic self-care checklist.

Next 24–48 Hours of Self-Care: Ensure adequate rest, sleep, and hydration.

Delay Major Life Decisions: Gently advise the guest to avoid making immediate, life-altering decisions (such as quitting a job, ending a marriage, or making large financial purchases) for a few days to weeks following an intense state, unless personal safety requires it.

Emergency Safety Net: Confirm whether the guest has a safe place to stay for the night and access to trusted friends, family, or emergency contacts.

### 3.3 Local Community Resources & Referrals

Provide access to reputable, legally compliant local support channels in Hong Kong.

Community Peer Support: Provide information on local community centers, wellness circles, or peer support groups.

Professional Mental Health Referrals: If the guest experiences persistent disorientation, dissociation, or severe emotional depression, provide contact details for recognized professional services in Hong Kong (e.g., The Hong Kong Psychological Society, Caritas Family Crisis Support Centre, Tung Wah Group of Hospitals Mental Health Services, The Samaritan Befrienders Hong Kong, or public hospital psychiatric outpatient clinics).

## 4. Volunteer Self-Care & Gift Guidelines

### 4.1 Pre- and Post-Duty Self-Care for Volunteers

Pre-Duty Preparation: Before entering a shift, volunteers should practice grounding, centering, meditation, or light journaling to clear their own mental space and establish a stable baseline.

Post-Duty De-roling & Processing: After concluding shifts or sessions, volunteers must prioritize self-care (e.g., meditation, somatic grounding, journaling) to process any absorbed emotional residue and smoothly transition back to personal life.

### 4.2 Handling Gifts from Guests

Redirecting to Material Donations: If a guest insists on expressing gratitude through a gift, volunteers should suggest replacing it with a material or monetary donation directly to the organization instead.

Keep it Light & Symbolic: Gifts should be limited to small tokens, snacks, or souvenirs. Cash, gift cards, and high-value luxury items are strictly prohibited.

Purely Appreciative (No Strings Attached): Gifts must only be given as post-service appreciation or seasonal greetings—never as a condition for securing services, priorities, or special treatment.

Open and Transparent: All exchanges should be conducted openly rather than through private or covert transactions.
